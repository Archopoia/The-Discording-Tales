Lineages

Hauts

High and Low Iqqars, unlike other separated and forgotten Races, possessed themselves as one in a great wind-guided surge. Together they descended upon the lands of the Beingsts to celebrate their union through an ecstatic triumph of every body.

High Iqqars (Ûtmart), tall and carved like rock, wear white or grey fur and deep grey eyes that mirror brute strength. From the highest cliffs they fiercely guarded, they long lived apart, violently repelling any who scaled their peaks. Their voyageurs-passepartouts, leaving no trace, crossed mountains and skies on cutting, colourful nephoptiques borne on the wind like kites, sketching cryptic messages in the clouds while they rode invisible whirlwinds on aèroclyde gliders to herald their foes. At last they left their fierce solitude, leaving conquered lands and peoples flattened in their wake.

  • Physique: Tall as rock carved, white or grey fur, deep grey eyes.
  • Traits: High Iqqars (once High-Erispiles) are physically mighty and hardy, but of lesser intelligence and wisdom.
  • Traditions: Their voyageurs-passepartouts left no trace, crossing mountains and skies on cutting nephoptiques.

Bas

High and Low Iqqars, unlike other separated and forgotten Races, possessed themselves as one in a great wind-guided surge. Together they descended upon the lands of the Beingsts to celebrate their union through an ecstatic triumph of every body.

Low Iqqars (Sää'm), with blonde or grey fur and ice-blue or piercing grey eyes, descend from ancients who lived alone in the arid depths of forgotten cliffs, ignoring others. Thanks to pneumophones carrying messages on the wind over long distances and their small méchaères (spring messengers), they emerged from the unknown - showing their maîtres-d'êtres first, knowing every foible of whatever crossed their path at a glance, just before drawing them in as if enchanted.

  • Physique: Medium stature, blonde or grey fur, ice-blue or piercing grey eyes.
  • Traits: Low Iqqars (once Low-Erispiles) are more intelligent and reasoned, but tend to be physically frailer with lesser charisma.
  • Traditions: Their maîtres-d'êtres know every foible of whatever crosses their path at a glance.
