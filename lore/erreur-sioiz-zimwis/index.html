The Error of the SIÔIZ and ZIMWIS

Page 120

  • That is why they sometimes name what was never established and judged by Wôm ( their efforts ), and other times what was never illuminated and revealed by Ô ( their origin ). But their plans never bear the fruits of Siwômiz they desire.
  • For thus pushed, the seventy - two fertile SIÔIZ ( Object / Image / Dream ) landless and with hallucinated forms from which bloomed Hôi - Iôs - Sôm - Môh, as well as the seventy - two subtle ZIMWIS ( Place / Concept / Notion ) headless and with liberated goods from which rotted iôH - sôI - môS - hôM, only grow by clothing themselves or dazzling with the radiance of Siwômiz's trees. Never are the Siôiz and Zimwis like its living forest, and never do the first nor the second bear its revered fruits - they alone so harmonized in taste and Rilic brilliance - But the story does not stop there...
  • Siwômiz, offended by each Siôiz and Zimwis born from jealousies of Ô's granaries, these shoots disinherited of its sap and seizing the milk of its roots, grows in reprimands tasteless or dull fruits in order to rot the Wo - of - Ô and Om - of - Ô, forced to feed on them to survive.
  • Thus these Rils ripening without Wôm's talents or Ò's gifts, never could satisfy them in their needs or desires - all then wandering between famine though as- suaged or satiety though disgusted.
  • Among Ô, Wo, Om and Wôm, only Ô eats fruits of Siwômiz, and al- ways seizes all harvests of her children without ever granting them a single one, for their own good.

Thus having no fruit to sate themselves, it happens that childish Wo-of-Ô, enticed by Ô's gifts mixed with her shining ribbons and so they might perhaps seize them before their parent's guard does, name these illuminated ones before Wôm with his diamond eyes can judge them and make them fruits.

Similarly, from insatiable curiosity, Wôm always looks everywhere seeking his prey illuminated by Ô's thread, whether present or not. Then and always because starved, it happens that pretty Wo-of-Ô, far too rejoiced by their baptizing power, amuse themselves predicting Wôm's so astonishing feasts and point and name wherever his eyes fall, wherever he does so, even without any of his resplendent prey, so they might perhaps leap upon them before him.

For from Siôiz with delirious silhouettes and Zimwis with delivered riches, Om-of-Ô succumb to the charms of the dazzling lights of the first contouring their thoughts, and frustrate themselves counting the stupifying effects of the second surrounding the airs. Seeing the homeless Siôiz, Om-of-Ô illuminate themselves through imagination and lose themselves through illusion, while thinking of headless Zimwis they heal themselves through chance and wound themselves through straying.

Thus during their dances at dusk and their songs wandering the evening through all Ô, the Siôiz sometimes reveal to Om-of-Ô numerous paths while the Zimwis sometimes point to them numerous remedies growing here and there along their way. But also and often, the first, without home, lead them astray from the true path until alienating them, and the second, without face, hide their true ingredients until poisoning them.
