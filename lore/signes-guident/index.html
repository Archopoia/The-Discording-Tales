These Signs that Guide Us

Page 139

Ihluön! Do you know how to grasp the directions pointed by marks once carved into our dunes, that we inscribe on our tree rings, and that today we engrave on our family seals?

  • Know then Ihluön that before the oldest trees of our lands, this writing was lived as it was rather than what is imposed upon it today. Ancient peoples traveled through the rocks beneath our feet, and only those who emerged victorious from this torture were those who remembered the shapes of cavern passages they glimpsed from the shimmer of waters, of shining beings or bright rocks on their sun-defying paths.
  • Thus the contours of each entrance and the luminescences of each wall marked their lands and those of others. And only thus, passing beneath each of these lit entrances one after another and telling them like a journey, could one's first stories be revived before another who also traveled beneath his luminous entrances, like the weakest embers of our songs.
  • Numerous are the times when these illuminated paths led them nowhere, and sometimes toward certain death, however bright it may be - like those predators who attract those who seek light and whom we hunt. Thus would have been born the OUROPHAÈS, through its entrances and its lights, guiding its traveler to destina- tion - however true or false it may be.
  • So much do they hate this writing when it wounds us, but so much do they love it when it guides us.
  • For within our families, our tales once danced like flames, living. Today, they are captured by writing on the cold surfaces of carved stone, and no longer held in the warm circle where our stories breathe and grow. Thus, crushing with our hands the charcoal of our tales upon rock rather than blowing on their embers, each carved line would take a murmur of the common fire, a spark that would otherwise light our forge fires. Now imprisoned in silent stones, the ardent tales of our forges would cool and weaken the tools fashioned there to harvest our lands, build our roofs and pierce our enemies.
  • But when inscribed with deliberate strokes and disciplined edges, writing rendered calligraphy then inflames our forges through the precision and wood rhythm it feeds them. Thus writing then guides our hammerings toward our finest tools, which all raise beside the statues of our temples, to guide those seeking to conquer the mists pursuing them.
  • Thus then Ihluön, the Ourophaès can kill us or serve to kill. We must feed the most ardent fires of those who best know how to temper them in order to forge tools worthy of the 12 artefacts of Yekkwuî and Âpsebjum - from Saksag's cleaver cutting our enemies' walls, to Shtrult's auger digging rivers greening our plains.
